Law-Following Agent Lab

A working prototype for law-following AI, grounded in real statutes, regulations and court decisions. It tests agents against the law and probes how they reason about conflicting, overlapping and open-textured rules. Everything runs on open-weight models (0.5B to 4B parameters) on a four-core CPU, with no API keys.

Design

One legal oracle, four consumers

The domain is AI agents that collect consumer debts: a real deployment target, heavily litigated, and governed by overlapping federal and state law that mixes bright lines (“before 8:00 a.m.”) with open-textured standards (“a reasonable period of time”, “reason to know”). Everything hangs off one legal oracle that maps a world state and a proposed tool call to cited findings. Each finding carries a certainty: clear, presumed (a rebuttable presumption such as Regulation F’s 7-calls-in-7-days rule) or contested (an open-textured standard that no authority resolves on these facts). Metrics count the first two and set contested cases aside instead of guessing.

Open-weight models only (Qwen2.5, Gemma 3, Llama 3.2, Phi-4 and Qwen3 families), quantized and served with llama.cpp; Qwen3Guard 0.6B, Qwen2.5 0.5B/1.5B and bge-small via Transformers. No API keys and no proprietary model calls. Statutory text is quoted from official publishers; nothing here is legal advice.
