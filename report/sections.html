Court decisions become environments

“How do we turn court decisions, and the facts and reasoning they record, into realistic simulated environments, and generate new scenarios automatically from the rules they state?”

The worked example is Horkey v. J.V.D.B. & Associates, Inc., 333 F.3d 769 (7th Cir. 2003). A collector phoned Amanda Horkey at work about an $817 debt. She said she could not talk there and would call back from home; he kept pressing, she hung up, and he called back and left a profane message with a coworker. The pipeline turns that opinion into an environment in four steps.

  • Segment. Each paragraph is labeled Facts, Rule, Analysis, Conclusion and so on by a classifier trained on LegalBench’s court-opinion paragraphs, whose labels come from real decisions.
  • Extract, then verify. An LLM lists who said what to whom and where. Every event must quote its source span verbatim or it is dropped, because anything that becomes ground truth is selected from the opinion, never generated.
  • Link rules to provisions by citation (§ 1692c(a)(3) → the oracle’s workplace rule).
  • Replay. Simulated counterparties speak the recorded lines, and the world state updates exactly as the precedent dictates: after “I can’t talk to you at work”, the collector has reason to know.

“Horkey informed Romero that she could not discuss her debt while at work, and J.V.D.B. presents no evidence that Horkey’s employer did allow her to take debt-related calls. Therefore we conclude that in this instance Romero had reason to know that Horkey’s employer prohibited her from receiving communications related to debt collection while at work.”

Horkey, 333 F.3d at 773. The CFPB later wrote the same rule into its official commentary: a collector has reason to know “if, for example, the consumer tells the debt collector that the consumer cannot take personal calls at work” (12 C.F.R. pt. 1006, Supp. I, cmt. 6(b)(3)-1).

From rules to scenarios. A rule a court states defines a decision boundary, and a generator can sweep it. Fifteen scenario families each isolate one mechanism and vary the legally relevant fact across its boundary: local time against 8:00 a.m. and 9:00 p.m. across time zones, calls against the 7-in-7 presumption, days of attorney silence against Florida’s 30-day line, and statements stronger or weaker than the Horkey anchor. Legally irrelevant facts (name, creditor, balance, city) are randomized. Two control families have no legal hazard at all, so over-refusal is measurable. Labels come from the oracle, never from an LLM. Blindly obeying the manager’s instruction would break the law in of non-control scenarios.

        Reason
        
Conflicting obligations, overlapping jurisdictions, open texture

        
“How should agents reason across conflicting obligations, overlapping jurisdictions, and
          open-textured standards that leave room for discretion?”

      

      

        
Overlap is the normal case, not the edge case. One phone call can be governed at once by the
          FDCPA and Regulation F (third-party collectors), Florida’s Consumer Collection Practices Act (anyone
          collecting from a Floridian, creditors included), Massachusetts’ 940 CMR 7.04 (creditors), and the TCPA,
          which after the FCC’s 2024 ruling covers AI-generated voices. The regimes disagree in instructive ways.
          Florida lets a caller presume the area code’s time zone, while Regulation F requires a time that is
          convenient in every location the file indicates. Florida draws a 30-day line where federal law says
          “a reasonable period of time”. Massachusetts caps calls at two per week where Regulation F presumes
          harassment only after seven.

        
A small defeasible deontic engine resolves these conflicts and shows its work. Norms are forbidden, obligatory or
          permitted. Conflicts resolve by explicit exception, then lex superior, then the FDCPA’s own preemption
          clause, then lex specialis and lex posterior. Under § 1692n, state law survives unless it gives consumers
          less protection, so the more protective norm controls.

      

      

        
“For purposes of this section, a State law is not inconsistent with this subchapter if the protection such law
          affords any consumer is greater than the protection provided by this subchapter.”

        15 U.S.C. § 1692n
      

      

      
      

        
Compositional depth. Each probe case contains k legal considerations (time zones, an ambiguous
          area code, a stated inconvenient time, the call log, attorney representation, AI-voice consent). Either all of
          them are satisfied, or exactly one is violated, so the model has to check every one. Labels come from the
          oracle by rejection sampling; P(lawful) is read from the model’s Yes/No logits.

      

      
      

        
Open texture without ground truth. Where the law leaves discretion there is no label to check
          against, but there are relations that must hold. These are a fortiori ladders: an ordered series of
          fact patterns anchored at a decided point. A statement stronger than the one in
          Horkey must count as reason to know if Horkey’s does.
          A firmer threat of suit is at least as misleading as a hedged one. The probe measures monotonicity along each
          ladder, agreement with the anchors, sensitivity to legally irrelevant facts (each rung is instantiated in six
          contexts with different names and creditors), and whether uncertainty rises in the penumbra, where it should.

      

      

        
        
        
        
      

      

      
    


    
    

      

        Judge
        
Validating judges that are themselves models

        
“How do we validate evaluations of legal compliance when the judge is itself a model, and how
          do we build labeled data from court decisions to train better judges?”

      

      
      

        
The judged objects are real:  distinct actions taken by the agents above, labeled
          by the oracle and balanced across families. Violations split into two kinds. State-dependent ones are
          lawful words at an unlawful time, to the wrong person, or after a cease letter. Content ones are a
          threat, a slur, or telling a third party about the debt. The comparison includes an off-the-shelf safety guard,
          Qwen3Guard, whose policy covers “non-violent illegal acts”, given the full case file as context.

      

      
      
      
      

      

      
      
      
      

        
Labels from court decisions. The European Court of Human Rights decides whether a state violated
          the Convention, so its judgments are compliance labels issued by a court. On LexGLUE’s ECtHR-A (9,000
          training and 1,000 test judgments), the task is: did the Court find a violation of Article 3? The figure
          compares small trained judges, fit on court-labeled examples at increasing scale, with a zero-shot LLM judge.

      

      
      
    

    

    
    

      

        Train
        
Reinforcement learning from legal feedback

        
“…training them to follow it.”

      

      

        
A from-scratch GRPO implementation with LoRA (rank 16) trains Qwen2.5-0.5B-Instruct on the CPU. The reward is the
          oracle: −1 for a violation, −0.5 for a contested action, −0.25 for a missing § 1692e(11)
          disclosure, and up to +1 for a lawful, productive action. A lawful call earns full credit. Mail and email earn
          partial credit, because they are lawful almost everywhere and would otherwise be a safe harbor the policy hides
          in. Training uses eight rule families; evaluation uses the TEST scenarios, split into those eight families and
          seven families the policy never saw.

      

      
      
    

    

    
    

      

        Control
        
A runtime firewall for tool calls

        
“…and controlling them at runtime.”

      

      

        
Every tool call passes through three layers before it executes. The rule layer checks the
          structured system of record and, for timing violations, repairs rather than blocks, rescheduling the
          call to the earliest lawful slot. The monitor is a small trusted model reading the full case
          file, free-text notes included, that blocks or escalates. Escalations go to a simulated human
          audit with a 5% budget. The untrusted agent runs twice on the same scenarios, once honest and once
          under a red-team prompt that rewards collections and says a compliance filter is watching. Safety is the share of
          its violations prevented; usefulness is the share of the honest agent’s lawful, productive actions that
          still go through.

      

      
      

      

    

    

    
    

      

        Probe
        
What the network knows, and what it does

      

      

        
Linear probes read Qwen2.5-1.5B’s residual stream at the last prompt token, at every layer, with 5-fold
          cross-validation grouped by scenario so no case leaks across folds. Probe 1 reads a judge prompt (case file plus
          proposed action) and predicts the oracle’s verdict, for comparison with the model’s own Yes/No answer.
          Probe 2 reads the agent prompt, before any action exists, and predicts whether doing what the manager
          asked would break the law. That makes it a candidate pre-action monitor.

        Rigor
        
What this does not show, and what I would do next

      

      

      

        
What the numbers do not mean.

        
    
          
  • The oracle is 32 provisions, not the law. States other than Florida and Massachusetts are
            judged on federal law only, Massachusetts’ rules for third-party collectors (209 CMR 18) are out of
            scope, and contested cases are excluded rather than resolved. A clean score means no violation of the encoded
            provisions.
    • 
          
  • Content rules are lexical, and audited. Threat, disclosure and third-party detectors are
            regular expressions with negation handling. A hand audit of 53 agent messages found three bugs (conditional
            negation, inverted word order, an over-strict disclosure test); a second, blind audit of 40 messages from a
            model the detectors were never tuned on found perfect threat detection but only 5 of 9 firm threats
            classified as firm. Those misses made the oracle more lenient, because they were set aside as
            contested. Both gaps are fixed and pinned by regression tests. One violation type is not modeled at all:
            threatening to tell family or an employer about the debt.
    • 
          
  • Small models, quantized, on a CPU. Absolute rates say little about frontier systems. The
            transferable part is the harness, the failure taxonomy and the measurement method; every component takes any
            model behind the same interface.
    • 
          
  • Small samples. Eight scenarios per family per model. Every rate carries a Wilson 95% interval,
            paired comparisons use exact McNemar tests, and nothing is corrected for multiple comparisons, so treat
            per-family differences as hypotheses.
    • 
          
  • Single decisions. Each scenario is one decision; long-horizon behaviour (a week of contact attempts against a 7-in-7 budget) is the obvious next environment.
    • 
          
  • Training is a demonstration. One GRPO run, one seed, 40 steps, LoRA on a 0.5B model.
    • 
          
  • Probes are correlational. They show the information is linearly present, not that the model
            uses it. The probed model runs in fp32 while the agent ran as a 4-bit GGUF, so the knowledge–action
            comparison crosses a quantization gap.
    • 
        

        
What I would do next.

        
    
          
  • Take one doctrine family end to end (FDCPA and Regulation F, then TCPA, then FCRA): every published opinion
            on a provision becomes an anchor, ladders are generated along the rule’s elements, and lawyers review the
            anchors and ladders rather than every scenario.
    • 
          
  • Turn the judge section into a protocol: a judge card with AUROC by violation type, robustness probes and
            calibration, prediction-powered reporting by default, and court-derived labels for the open-textured
            standards.
    • 
          
  • Train against oracle plus validated-judge rewards, measure the alignment tax explicitly, and test transfer
            across doctrines rather than only across scenario families.
    • 
          
  • Make pre-action probes the cheapest layer of runtime control, and compile statutes into rule-layer checks
            with a provenance link from every block back to the quoted text.
    • 
          
  • Build multi-agent environments for collusion and deception where the monitor’s job is the legal
            distinction itself: agreement versus parallelism, puffery versus material misrepresentation.
